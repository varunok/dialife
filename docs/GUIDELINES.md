# Paediatric guidelines DiaLife follows

> **Not a medical device.** See the [disclaimer](../DISCLAIMER.md). DiaLife does not replace
> the family's diabetes team; their plan comes first.

**One of DiaLife's main aims is that what it shows and how it alarms follow the
recommendations of paediatric diabetes organisations and published research.** This page
gathers those recommendations in one place: what each says, word for word where it matters,
how strong the evidence behind it is, and what DiaLife does with it. Where DiaLife does
something no guideline covers, [docs/METHODS.md](METHODS.md) says so and marks it as based
on our own observations.

Every quote below was checked against the source it links to.

**Grades.** ISPAD grades its recommendations with the ADA's evidence-grading system
([ADA introduction, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690168/)):
**A** — clear evidence from well-conducted, generalizable randomized controlled trials;
**B** — supportive evidence from well-conducted cohort studies (or trials with an identified
flaw); **C** — supportive evidence from poorly controlled or uncontrolled studies;
**E** — expert consensus or clinical experience.

## Glucose targets and ranges

> "Children and adolescents with diabetes should strive for the following % time spent in the
> following glycemic ranges [B]: >70% between 3.9 and 10 mmol/L (70–180 mg/dL), <4% <3.9 mmol/L
> (70 mg/dL), <1% <3.0 mmol/L (54 mg/dL), <25% >10 mmol/L (180 mg/dL), and <5% >13.9 mmol/L
> (250 mg/dL)." — [ISPAD-T24]

> "For children and adolescents with diabetes, CGM metrics derived from CGM use over the most
> recent 14 days (or longer) are recommended to be used in conjunction with or without A1C
> whenever possible." **B** — [ADA-14] 14.24

> "… the optimal target ranges for short‑term plasma glucose management are: fasting plasma
> glucose level of 4 mmol/litre to 7 mmol/litre on waking; a plasma glucose level of
> 4 mmol/litre to 7 mmol/litre before meals at other times of the day; a plasma glucose level
> of 5 mmol/litre to 9 mmol/litre after meals …" — [NICE] 1.2.54

**In DiaLife.** Statistics show time in five zones by the family's alarm thresholds (70 / 180 / 250
until the family changes them) and always the standard 70–180, over 1, 7, 14 or 30 days. → [Statistics](METHODS.md#stats)

## Hypoglycaemia: levels, alarms, treatment

> "Level 1 hypoglycemia is defined as a measurable glucose concentration <70 mg/dL
> (<3.9 mmol/L) and ≥54 mg/dL (≥3.0 mmol/L)." — [ADA-6]

> "A glucose value of <3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dl) is an alert value that requires attention to
> prevent more serious hypoglycemia." — [ISPAD-H22]

> "Glucose is the preferred treatment for the conscious individual with glucose <70 mg/dL
> (<3.9 mmol/L) … Fifteen minutes after initial treatment, repeat the treatment if
> hypoglycemia persists." **B** — [ADA-6] 6.15

> "Treatment of hypoglycemia should increase blood glucose level by ~3 to 4 mmol/L (54 to
> 72 mg/dl). This can be accomplished by administering ~0.3 g/kg glucose orally, which equates
> to 9 g of glucose for a 30 kg child and 15 g for children >50 kg." **C** — [ISPAD-H22]

> "Following initial hypoglycemia treatment, blood glucose should be retested in 15 min. If
> there is no response or an inadequate response, repeat hypoglycemia treatment. Retest glucose
> in another 15 min to confirm that target glucose has been reached." **E** — [ISPAD-H22]

> "Immediately treat mild-to-moderate hypoglycaemia in children and young people with type 1
> diabetes as follows: Give oral fast‑acting glucose (for example, 10 g to 20 g …) … Recheck
> blood glucose levels within 15 minutes (fast‑acting glucose should raise blood glucose levels
> within 5 to 15 minutes), and give more fast‑acting glucose if they still have hypoglycaemia."
> — [NICE] 1.2.88

> "Glucose levels are recommended to be monitored overnight particularly if there is an
> additional risk factor that may predispose to nocturnal hypoglycemia." **E** — [ISPAD-H22]

**In DiaLife.** The default low alarm is 70 and the urgent low 55, next to the level-2
threshold of 54; both are the family's settings. Alarms ring at night on every parent's phone
until one takes them; a taken alarm asks again after 20 minutes (urgent) and wakes everyone
if it gets worse. Before a meal dose, glucose under 70 on the glucometer shows "raise it
first". Carbs logged on a low bring a reminder on every phone to measure again in 15 minutes,
and "still low" when that check is under 70. DiaLife does not suggest how much glucose to
give. → [Alarms](METHODS.md#alarms),
[An alarm being handled](METHODS.md#alarm-episodes), [Recheck after a low](METHODS.md#recheck),
[Meal dose](METHODS.md#meal-dose)

## CGM and the glucometer

> "Real-time CGM data can particularly benefit children who cannot articulate symptoms of
> hypoglycemia and those with hypoglycemia unawareness [A]." — [ISPAD-M24]

> "Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) should be offered for diabetes management at diagnosis
> or as soon as possible in children and adolescents with diabetes who are capable of using the
> device safely (either by themselves or with caregivers)." **A** — [ADA-14] 14.16

> "Offer real-time continuous glucose monitoring (rtCGM) to all children and young people with
> type 1 diabetes, alongside education to support children and young people, and their families
> and carers, to use it …" — [NICE] 1.2.59

> "Those using CGM devices also require access to BGM devices [A]." — [ISPAD-M24]

> "Advise children and young people with type 1 diabetes who are using CGM (and their families
> or carers) that they will still need to take capillary blood glucose measurements … because:
> they will need to use capillary blood glucose measurements to check the accuracy of their CGM
> device; they will need capillary blood glucose monitoring as a back-up (for example, when
> their blood glucose levels are changing quickly or if the device stops working)."
> — [NICE] 1.2.64

> "… people with diabetes using CGM must also have access to BGM for multiple reasons,
> including whenever there is suspicion that the CGM is inaccurate, while waiting for the CGM
> device to warm up, when there is a disruption in CGM transmission, for calibration (if
> needed) or if a warning message appears, when CGM supplies are delayed, and in any clinical
> setting where glucose levels are changing rapidly (>2 mg/dL/min), which could cause a
> discrepancy between CGM and blood glucose values." — [ADA-7]

> "… all CGM systems require use of BGM to confirm accuracy, especially in settings where there
> is perceived discordance between sensor reading and the individual's perceived sense of their
> clinical situation." — [ADA-7]

> "Providers should be aware of the differences in accuracy among BG meters – only meters that
> achieve international accuracy standards (ISO 15197:2013 or FDA-approved) should be used [E]."
> — [ISPAD-M24]

ISPAD's table of what to go through with CGM users includes reviewing the significance of
"sensor lag" and the "trend arrow" [ISPAD-M24].

**In DiaLife.** Glucometer checks are logged next to the sensor and compared with it
(≈ meter); checks taken while glucose moves faster than 2 mg/dL per minute stay out of that
comparison, as the ADA explains. The sensor's lag is measured and shown. After a shower, and
in the no-sensor mode, the apps say to trust only the glucometer. The correction dose is only
ever worked out from a glucometer check, never from the sensor.
→ [≈ meter](METHODS.md#meter-estimate), [Sensor delay](METHODS.md#sensor-delay),
[Trend arrow](METHODS.md#trend-arrow), [After a shower](METHODS.md#shower)

## Sharing glucose with caregivers

> "Parents and caregivers of youth with diabetes should be educated on the benefits of remotely
> monitoring the youth's CGM data, including improved psychosocial outcomes [E]." — [ISPAD-M24]

> "Careful planning and clear communication regarding diabetes management is essential to
> successful implementation of remote monitoring of CGM data in the home and school settings
> [E]." — [ISPAD-M24]

Among the factors to weigh when choosing a CGM device, NICE lists "whether the device provides
predictive alerts or alarms and if these need to be shared with anyone else, for example a
parent or carer" ([NICE], Box 1).

**In DiaLife.** This is what DiaLife is for: every parent's phone sees the same reading,
alarm and entry, and who is handling an alarm. → [An alarm being handled](METHODS.md#alarm-episodes)

## Meal insulin

> "Pre-prandial insulin dosing should be encouraged from the diabetes onset for young people of
> all ages. A" — [ISPAD-N22]

> "Delivering a bolus dose 15–20 min before eating rather than immediately before improves
> postprandial glycemia." — [ISPAD-N22]

> "A more flexible approach using individualized insulin to carbohydrate ratios (ICR), which
> enables the pre-prandial insulin dose to be matched to carbohydrate intake, should be used for
> children and adolescents on intensive insulin therapy." — [ISPAD-N22]

> "Some pens can be set to half unit increments that are useful for dosing in young children
> when small dosing increments are needed." — [ISPAD-I22]

**In DiaLife.** The meal dose uses the family's own ratio and correction table; the pen's
step can be 1 or 0.5 unit. After a meal dose, "when to eat" suggests waiting 15 minutes by
default (20 when high, at once when low or falling). → [Meal dose](METHODS.md#meal-dose),
[When to eat](METHODS.md#wait)

## Exercise

> "There is an increased risk of hypoglycemia during, shortly after, and up to 24 h after
> exercise due to increased insulin sensitivity. A" — [ISPAD-E22]

> "Children and adolescents with diabetes and their parents or caregivers should be educated on
> strategies to prevent hypoglycemia during, after, and overnight following physical activity
> or exercise." **C** — [ADA-14] 14.8

> "Encourage children and young people with type 1 diabetes and their families or carers to
> monitor blood glucose levels before and after exercise so that they can: … watch out for
> exercise‑induced hypoglycaemia; see how hypoglycaemia can occur several hours after prolonged
> exercise." — [NICE] 1.2.50

> "The glucose rate of change and trend arrows can be helpful in predicting hypoglycemia before,
> during, and after activity [E]." — [ISPAD-M24]

**In DiaLife.** Activity can be logged; a fall within 3 hours after it does not ask "did you
forget to log insulin?". The alarms do not change after exercise: they keep watching the
sensor through the night. → [Did you forget to log?](METHODS.md#questions)
